28 DAYS

A free game about a first period and the month that follows. Pack your bag, go to health class, handle the leaks, and learn how the menstrual cycle works by living through one.

Play the game Classroom Edition

Runs in your browser. Nothing to install, no account. You need a keyboard.

THE GAME

You play Ruby. Her first period starts in the middle of health class.

From there you guide her through one full cycle. A simulation tracks her flow, cramps, energy, mood and cravings. Forget to pack a pad and she leaks. Pack well and the day goes smoothly.

Home: pack

Your bag holds only six things. Pads, a snack, spare pants, a swimsuit: you choose each morning.

School: learn

Health class with Dr. Flo. A short lesson and a pop quiz every school day, and a unit test at the end.

Corner Mart: buy

Buy your own supplies on a budget, and find out what each product is for.

Every night: track

Log flow, cramps and mood in a cycle tracker. The better you track, the better you can plan.

All four phases of the cycle

The game covers the whole month, including the weeks without a period. Four hormones show up as characters to explain what is happening inside.

FOR TEACHERS

A Classroom Edition for middle school health classes

The Classroom Edition is a separate version of the game made for school use. It is written for the whole class: three boys in the story ask questions and learn alongside Ruby.

  • Start on any day, so one day fits in one class period
  • A printable teacher guide with pacing plans and discussion questions
  • An answer key for all 23 quiz questions
  • A one-page student worksheet
  • A glossary with a labeled body diagram
  • Sound starts off
Teacher guide Open the Classroom Edition

What it covers

The biology of the menstrual cycle, period products and hygiene, cramps, PMS, mood changes, cycle tracking, and when to see a doctor.

What it doesn't

Sexual activity, contraception, or pregnancy. Pregnancy is mentioned only to explain why the lining of the uterus builds up each cycle.

Please have a school nurse or health teacher review it before using it with students. It has not yet been reviewed by a medical professional.

THE FACTS

Every fact shows where it came from

The game has 31 "Did you know?" facts, five health lessons and a glossary. Each one was checked against pages from the CDC, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG), the NIH and a few others. Where sources disagree, the game uses ACOG's number.

The full list of claims, sources and corrections is public: read the fact check.

28 Days is not medical advice. If you have questions about your own body, talk to a parent or guardian, a school nurse, or a doctor.

ABOUT

Made by a student

28 Days was created by Swara Palanivel and first presented at High School I/O 2026, a student hackathon. It is built in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no game engine and no image files: every tile and character is pixel art drawn by code. It was built with Claude Code, an AI coding assistant.
